Molehill Madness privacy policy

Molehill Madness is a game for four people. It is built to know as little about them as it can, because the safest way to look after somebody's information is not to have any.

Last updated 31 August 2026. Published by Inthrall, New Zealand.

This policy covers the Molehill Madness game on Android and on desktop, and the service it talks to in order to let people play together.

The short version

There is no account with your name on it. There is no advertising, and there are no advertising identifiers. There is no analytics, no tracking across apps or websites, and no data sold or shared with anybody for their own purposes. Playing on one device with people in the same room sends nothing anywhere at all.

What we hold, and why

Playing with people who are not in the room needs a service to pass turns between you. It holds the following, and nothing else.

WhatWhy it exists
A random account identifier and a secret So the game can prove it is the same device coming back to a match in progress. It is generated on your device and is not derived from anything about you or your hardware.
An age band: under 13, 13 to 15, or 16 and over To apply the right rules to what is offered. It is a band rather than a date of birth, because a band is what the rules need and a date of birth is more than that.
When the account was created and last seen To find and remove accounts nobody is using.
An email address, only if you choose to add one So an adult can get back to their matches on a new device. It is optional, it is offered only to accounts in the 16 and over band, and it is refused for anybody else.
Match data: a five letter game code, a seed, the plans each player submits each round, and the result This is the game itself. A plan is a short block of numbers describing where a mole walked and what it threw; it is meaningless outside the match it belongs to.
Emotes sent during a match The only way players can communicate, and deliberately the only way.

What we never collect: your name, your date of birth, your location, your contacts, your photos, your microphone or camera, your device identifiers, an advertising identifier, or anything about what else is on your device. There is no free text chat in the game, so there is nothing for anybody to type to anybody else.

Children

The game is made to be suitable for all ages, and the age band above is the whole of what it does with age. Nothing is taken away from a younger player except the ability to be matched with strangers, which needs an account and is offered only above a threshold. There is no advertising to anybody, of any age, and no data is collected for advertising purposes.

Who else sees it

Nobody. The information above is held on servers we run, is used only to let the game work, and is not sold, rented, or shared with any third party for their own purposes. We do not use analytics services or advertising networks.

Where the game is installed from a store, that store collects its own information under its own policy, including crash reports when the game stops unexpectedly. A crash report says what the program was doing when it failed. We read those to fix faults, and they contain none of the information in the table above.

How long it is kept

Match data is kept while the match is playable and for a while afterwards, so that a result can still be looked at. There is no automatic deletion of finished matches yet; when there is, this section will say how long. An account is kept while it is in use. Ask us to remove yours and we will delete the account, its age band, any email address on it, and the matches it took part in.

Getting your data, or getting rid of it

Write to the address below and ask. We will tell you what is held against your account, or delete it, whichever you asked for.

Because an account is a random identifier rather than a name, we need something that ties the request to it: write from the email address linked to the account if there is one, or tell us a game code you played in and roughly when. Without one of those we cannot tell which account is yours, and guessing would mean deleting somebody else's.

Deleting the game from your device removes everything held on the device itself, including the account identifier. That does not by itself remove anything from the service, so ask us as well if that is what you want.

The steps, and what exactly goes, are on the account deletion page.

Security

Traffic between the game and the service is encrypted in transit. The secret that proves a device owns its account is stored on that device. We keep as little as possible, which is the strongest protection available to a game this size: information nobody holds cannot be lost.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and the previous versions stay readable in the game's public source repository, so the history of what was promised is not something anybody has to take our word for.
